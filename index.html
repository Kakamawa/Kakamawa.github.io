Reservoir Simulation & Subsurface Energy Systems

Studying fluids, heat, and fractures in complex geological reservoirs.

My research applies reservoir simulation to subsurface energy systems, with work spanning underground hydrogen storage, CO₂ storage and transport, geothermal energy, and enhanced geothermal systems.

Research

Underground Hydrogen Storage

H₂–CH₄ dispersion and mixing in depleted gas reservoirs, including heterogeneity, gravity segregation, and cyclic injection and production.

CO₂ Storage & Transport

CO₂–CH₄ dispersion in depleted gas reservoirs, with emphasis on heterogeneity, channeling, gravity, and water saturation.

Geothermal Energy

Numerical assessment of heat extraction from deep geopressured reservoirs, including the Wilcox Formation along the Texas Gulf Coast.

Enhanced Geothermal Systems

Fracture-controlled flow and heat extraction using reservoir simulation, EDFM, hydraulic-fracture representations, and fracture-network models.

Research connection

Reservoir simulation across subsurface energy systems

Across these applications, I use reservoir engineering and numerical simulation to understand how fluids, heat, and fractures behave in heterogeneous subsurface systems.

About

I am a PhD researcher in the Hildebrand Department of Petroleum and Geosystems Engineering at The University of Texas at Austin. My work builds on reservoir engineering and numerical simulation and applies those tools to subsurface energy systems.

My research interests include reservoir simulation, underground hydrogen storage, CO₂ storage and transport, geothermal energy, enhanced geothermal systems, multiphase flow, thermal transport, and fracture-controlled flow.
